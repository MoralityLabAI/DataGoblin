# Bounded candidate repair as an MMS allocation primitive

Source: https://github.com/MoralityLabAI/LoopedTransformers/tree/5c37c52532a5799f1c54e1279b532f8904e983ee/experiments/logrank_bpd_14.

14 uses 36 new worlds, 12 for temperature calibration and 24 for evaluation, and shared 24-unit verifier traces. At d48/.15 noise, baseline BP Brier .09613, single-frame repair .04077, random eight-frame repair .02337; mean decode time 6.8/20.3/95.9 ms. Random repair improves just five of eight paired worlds (two numerical near-ties and one material degradation). At .35, every uncalibrated d48 repair is worse than uniform Brier .25. The eight-frame consensus guard reduces d48/.15 coverage to zero. These are development comparisons, not a deployment recommendation.

Separate local decoder budgets from verifier-call budgets. Cache candidate masks, exact integer likelihood counters where feasible, frame maps and deduplicated state IDs. Preserve an explicit unvisited partition with admissible weight bound U; a retained weight W>0 gives posterior mass lower bound W/(W+U), and event-weight W_A gives interval [W_A/(W+U),(W_A+U)/(W+U)]. Neither a small residual nor agreement across gauges certifies the interval without U. Account for measured RSS, time, candidate evaluations and verifier costs; current scratch-array counts are lower bounds, not a RAM cap.

Next prospective protocol: compare fixed BP, single-neighborhood repair, matched random multi-frame repair and branch-and-bound omitted-weight control. Start with exact small quotients; hold out roots and distinct generator families; report coverage, correctness, proper scoring, probability interval width and all resource costs. A controller chooses between spending compute, acquiring a costed observation and abstaining. Bound branch likelihood using determined factors exactly and unresolved factors conservatively. No native DataGoblin integration, learned controller, or confirmation release is claimed by this note.
