# One-bit receipt experiment

Freeze before scoring. Compare C (two candidate options) with D (evidence and revisable proposal) in small tied dense networks. Both start from identical numerical parameters per seed. A stateless MLP and finite Bayes controls establish how much the recurrence buys. Only the committed binary symbol crosses from the producer into a fresh call to the receiver. Every goal buys exactly one independent environment check. No evaluator, registry or compiler runs inside the network.

Train each arm on the same ordered sequence of complete finite games. In parity games the producer sees three bits and the deployment prior. The receiver sees this prior, two directions, a verified side bit and the uploaded bit. Optimize the exact Brier expectation over the two possible hard symbols, with an auxiliary Brier risk using the Bayes receiver for that stochastic channel. This gives encoder credit for information a still-learning receiver cannot use. Training sees target outcomes, never an optimal encoder or a switch label. Test by thresholding the producer once per world and directly scoring the actual decoder. Also refit an ideal finite receiver analytically to each frozen code to distinguish encoder mistakes from readout mistakes. This second number is diagnostic.

Use seeds 137, 163 and 191. Give every model 1,200 parity updates, then adapt that checkpoint for 600 updates on training-family LDT games. Use Adam, LR 0.003, equal game weighting and two shared visits for C and D. Train parity at priors 0, .025, .05, .10, .15, .35, .75 and 1. Test exact code optimality by exhaustive enumeration, accepting any value-equivalent partition and its complement. Count switch success on a seed only if the frozen encoder reaches the optimal value at both .07 and .08. Report the tie and the wider grid as diagnostics. The grid only brackets a transition.

Generate structurally distinct fresh LDT families from an independent evaluation seed after all training. Score parity checkpoints and adapted checkpoints. Report equal-family average Brier risk and corrected/spoiled binary decisions relative to the ideal side-only decision (ties predict one). Report exact side-only risk, Bayes risk of the best primitive parity receipt for each game, and the unrestricted one-bit optimum. The primitive control is allowed to choose its parity separately in each game and to fit an ideal receiver. Report family-level results so one large family cannot dominate.

A 10B nomination requires bracket success on all three seeds, positive realized gain over side-only at both bracket points on every seed, and lower adapted LDT Brier than the MLP and best primitive control on the family-balanced aggregate on every seed. Require a paired advantage over the other architecture on all three LDT aggregates, and no test family with mean negative incremental gain over side-only. Failure means withhold the nomination. No tuning against fresh families and no replacement of failed seeds. This pilot assesses a mechanism and cannot estimate language-model scaling performance.
