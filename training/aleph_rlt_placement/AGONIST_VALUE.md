# What should a small challenger contribute to a larger model's synthetic corpus?

Treat a proposed challenge as an experiment with a verifiable outcome. The large builder supplies a claim or candidate, the small model selects a test, a checker records its outcome, and a separately reset student consumes the resulting records. These four roles can have different compute budgets. A correction and a verified confirmation both change a student's evidence. Give the challenger a budget for queries, including fruitless queries. Reserve another generator, grader custody and a fixed scoring rule for evaluation.

## Two competing theories and a revision

The error-count theory assigns a challenge its probability of exposing a builder error. It predicts that a reliable source of certain mistakes is an excellent data collector. A second theory assigns it the expected reduction in downstream Bayes risk. It predicts that the value of a receipt depends on what is already in the ledger and on which decisions the student must make. Both theories can be right about their own observable. Test their opposing predictions about *fresh student utility* with identical query, data and training budgets.

For a ledger D, a hidden fresh deployment input X and binary outcome Y, let p_D(X) be the conditional mean of Y. With a correctly specified Bayesian student and Brier loss, the expected gain from a verified transcript Z is

    E[(Y - p_D(X))² - (Y - p_{D,Z}(X))² | D]
      = E[(p_{D,Z}(X) - p_D(X))² | D].

Condition on D, X and Z and the residual cross-term has mean zero. The policy selecting Z sees public information only. This is the standard conditional variance identity. Our design choice is to apply it to *the next student's target decisions*, and to score a bundle of experiments against equal-cost rivals. A finite budget makes lookahead matter: two individually worthless checks can jointly identify the target.

### A game a small model can play

Draw three independent fair hidden bits. The target is the parity of the first two. A verified probe may reveal the first bit, the second, an independent nuisance bit, or a constant zero. The builder always says one on that last probe and zero on the other three. Buy two distinct probes with equal costs, then train a fresh finite posterior student and predict the unqueried parity. Uniformly randomize tied acquisition rules. This deliberate stress test favors reasoning about complementarities. It does not establish how often real corpora exhibit them.

More generally let the world be uniform over a binary vector space, each noiseless probe return a linear functional, and the target direction be t. The posterior after probes A is uniform on a coset of ker(A). If t lies in the row span of A, its label is fixed. Otherwise there exists v in ker(A) with t·v=1, so pairing worlds with their translates by v proves a fair posterior. Thus the Brier gain for a previously uncertain target is exactly 1/4 when the purchased span first contains it, and zero otherwise. For a distribution over targets sum their weights. An additional independent probe can yield zero target value. An agent can select a missing direction knowing the experiment descriptions alone. Two agents adding left and right each have singleton value zero and together deliver 1/4. If splitting this one toy payoff symmetrically between them, each earns 1/8, regardless of upload order.

The executable census in `agonist_value.py` compares rank predictions against a separately enumerated posterior over worlds and observed transcripts on 448 probe-bank/target combinations. Its four acquisition rules give gains of 0 (maximize teacher errors), 1/24 (uniform), 1/12 (maximize total posterior support reduction), and 1/4 (target-span lookahead). The error collector finds 1.5 expected mistakes against the target policy's one. Thus the two quantities can rank collectors in opposite order.

### Revision after a rival explanation survives

The noiseless span theory implicitly assumes a compatible student and checker. Replace perfect labels with independent flips of probability e. A parity of two observations has signed reliability (1−2e)². A calibrated Bayesian student has expected Brier gain (1−2e)⁴/4. A student that believes every received label instead has gain 1/4−2e(1−e). At e=1/4 the same two records yield +1/64 and −1/8 respectively. A student that stores separate literal keys yields zero transfer to the new key even at zero noise. These are executable counterexamples to using span alone as a data valuation rule.

For any actual trained probability predictor f and its optimal conditional mean p, squared loss decomposes into irreducible risk plus E[(f−p)²]. Therefore the actual expected gain equals the oracle gain plus the old student's excess risk minus the new student's excess risk. This gives the revised prediction: a query helps if its target information exceeds the extra estimation/optimization error incurred while fitting it. Randomize bundles across fresh copies of the same student, control optimizer steps and batch sizes, average over held roots, and vary the student and reliability independently. Compare with an error-count collector, uniform sampling, and a simple non-neural acquisition rule. Correlated verifier errors break the independent-flip expression.

## A second test on the pinned Lean receipts

This development comparison is separate from the placement tournament. Freeze the following choices before computing policy scores. Use the existing graded candidate views and the held-structure goals. The student is a small beta-binomial ranker with independent Beta(1,1) buckets derived from the first encoded tactic token and the tactic's four-token length band capped at three. The training candidate distribution supplies unlabeled bucket weights. Use the policy budget to reveal cached outcomes from training goals only. Unavailable checks consume a slot. The acquisition policies are uniformly random, highest expected rejection (1−p), and highest predicted Brier reduction w p(1−p)/(n+1)², with n the current beta mass. The last expression follows from the two possible posterior mean updates. All three policies use the same prior and tie permutation per seed. Compare at 8, 32 and 96 checks, seeds 137/163/191. A zero-check student establishes the lexical reference. Report held candidate Brier loss and actual held goals with a verified success among the first four attempts. These endpoints can disagree. Goal-independent buckets and shared-family effects violate independence; preserve evidence against the approximation. The archived proposals are a stand-in for the builder; this replay measures no frontier-model interaction. Wall-clock and generator transfer require a separate experiment.

### What actually happened on held structures

At 96 cached checks, uniform sampling reached mean held Brier 0.04234 and proved 4, 0 and 4 of 28 goals. Rejection mining reached 0.13041 and proved 8, 8 and 4. The target-weighted policy reached 0.03549 and proved 8, 4 and 4. The zero-check student scored 0.25 and proved 8 on every seed using lexical ties. The fixed proposal pool contains a checked solution for 16 goals. At eight checks the target policy already led on mean Brier, 0.06374 against uniform's 0.07506 and rejection's 0.13735. There are seven held structural families. These are descriptive measurements on a public pack.

The supervised ranker learned that most candidates fail. Better probabilities for those negatives did not reliably put a successful tactic in the first four. This is evidence against using average candidate Brier as a proxy for the value of a proof-training corpus. A more specific decision target is the marginal probability of finding **at least one** verified proof within budget, where two successful tactics for the same goal can be redundant. Under a joint posterior over candidate grades, let U(B) be the chance that a four-tactic set B contains a valid tactic. Conditional decision value is the expected new maximum U minus its current maximum. If the same B stays optimal after every possible label, this oracle gain is zero by the tower property. Calibrated candidate probabilities can still change. The next prospectively testable collector should seek checks that change the best slate, preserve the original ranker as an available policy, and report fresh-family checked goals per total call. Dependencies among candidate outcomes must enter the joint estimate.

Reproduce with `python training/aleph_rlt_placement/lean_acquisition.py --pack runs/placement/mixed --out runs/placement/lean-acquisition.json`. The receipt records every purchased training goal and proposal index. The actor uses training labels only after selection.

The [follow-up decision certificate experiment](SLATE_CERTIFICATES.md) compared two actual four-tactic slates per public goal. Checking the candidates on which the two slates differ first certified the same 84 comparisons in 328 calls versus 492 exhaustive union calls. It tied a learned-prior optimal decision tree on each seed. This measures a concrete contribution available to a small collector with access to shared verifier receipts.

## Interface for a network of small agents

A public prompt gives a claim, the current ledger, an intervention menu, a target family and remaining cost. The actor names a test, two predicted outcomes under competing mechanisms, what would falsify its preference, and a cost. The hub freezes that receipt, then a separate checker reveals an outcome. Link all reused records to their origins. Give every participating policy identical access to shared parent receipts. For a nonlinear recurrent core, the clock-order game in [CLOCK_ORDER_GYM.md](CLOCK_ORDER_GYM.md) supplies verifiable local edges. A centralized policy with access to all clock endpoints can evaluate the potentials directly in linear work. Include it as a baseline. A decentralized contributor earns scientific credit for a blind, reproducible forecast on a new root or a cheaper useful acquisition. Algebraic consequences keep their parents' provenance.

The exact finite game extends EvalMath's mechanism-separation setup with a deployment distribution, a student and an acquisition budget. For real training the primary estimand is the difference in fresh held risk between *policies*, including their whole acquisition and verification costs. A batch-level randomized comparison estimates that difference without assuming a mechanistic model. Split by parent goal or generator, prohibit receipt or label leakage, and report failed checks, abstentions and unusable proposals. If only the oracle improves, preserve that as a transfer failure.
