# Native courier action equivalence and bounded verification

Fix a world $W=(G,O,P,d,e,F_0,F_1)$. The directed arcs of $G$ are open or permit-gated. $O$, $P$, $d$, and $e$ are the issuing offices, pickup sites, desk, and entry. $F_i$ is the set of outlets for evidence identity $i$. The two identities are independent by construction. All outlets in $F_i$ attest the same source.

The immutable physical state is $s=(x,p,c,E)$. Location $x$ is a site, $p$ is a Boolean permit, cargo $c$ is waiting, carrying, or delivered, and $E\subseteq\{0,1\}$ is the set of attested identities. The initial state is $(e,0,\mathrm{waiting},\varnothing)$. A typed move follows one admissible arc. Issue is legal at $O$ with no permit. Collect is legal at $P$ with waiting cargo. Attest $i$ inserts $i$ at $F_i$ if it is absent. Deliver requires carrying cargo, $x=d$, and $E=\{0,1\}$. Delivered states have no primitive successors. The endpoint is delivery followed by an accepted STOP. A permit is required only when a route uses a gated arc. STOP costs one verifier call and zero primitive actions.

## Objectives

Let $A(s)$ be the legal primitive actions and $T(s,a)$ their deterministic transition. For the primitive certificate schedule, let $Q(s,h,b;a)$ be the best lexicographic value $(\text{verified completion},-\text{primitive length},-\text{call count})$ with first action $a$, at most $h$ actions and $b$ verifier calls. An accepted STOP contributes $(1,0,-1)$. Each legal primitive with $h>0,b>0$ evaluates the successor value $V(T(s,a),h-1,b-1)$ and debits one length and one call. A failure sentinel ranks below all successful continuations. Define $V(s,h,b)=\max_a Q(s,h,b;a)$, including STOP when feasible.

The exact shortest action set is $A^*(s,h,b)=\arg\max_a Q(s,h,b;a)$ on states with feasible full completion. The primitive tariff is uniform, so all shortest successes have the same call count. The budget-aware action set $A^+(s,h,b)$ includes every first action admitting full verified continuation under both caps. It can contain longer alternatives. A reverse shortest-path traversal over independently cross-checked native successors computes both sets. It omits unreachable successors and compares one plus successor distance with $h$ and two plus that distance with $b$. The second increment accounts for STOP. These labels refer to the physical transition graph. The learned rollout additionally masks states it has already visited on ordinary moves.

The training stream uses one matched row per visited state per root across two extreme shortest demonstrations. Canonical supervision uses the smallest action ID. The hashed control uses SHA-256 of the typed state and world identity, reduced modulo $|A^*|$. The joint loss is

$$
\mathcal L=-\log\sum_{a \in A_{\mathrm{input}}(s)}w_a p_\theta(a\mid s,h,b).
$$

Use weight 1 on the designated singleton, all of $A^*$, or all of $A^+$, respectively. Soft supervision assigns each feasible first action weight $2^{-(m_a+k_a-m_*-k_*)/2}$, where $m$ and $k$ are primitive actions and verification calls on its cheapest certified continuation. Infeasible actions have weight 0. The maximum weight is 1. A two-unit increase in combined cost halves the weight. Only public facts, current obligations and counters, and the local allowed-action mask enter the pointer. Future continuation values enter training weights and the explicit reference.

## Transactions and reachable-state equivalence

The pinned MeTTa relation authorizes each physical transition. Evidence rules authorize attestations and guard delivery. A macro is a typed tuple of primitives whose length fits the remaining horizon. A single native query recursively derives its whole proof, including the ordered intermediate states and final state. The session publishes the final state only after exact agreement with the independent projection. The receipt binds the rules and world facts, query, actions, initial and final states, and each proof step. A rejection spends its query, emits no accepted-step digests, and leaves the physical state and committed route unchanged. Macro depth bounds native proof work as well as candidate search. A query on a length-four macro can do more work than a one-step query.

For fixed $W$, tariff, and bounded macro vocabulary, two states are equivalent at $(h,b)$ when their languages of successful certified suffixes are equal. A suffix includes its action tokens and resource charges. The relation preserves optimal success and cost by taking the minimum realized cost over the same suffix language. Recursion partitions nonempty suffixes by the first certificate and reduces both budgets by its charges. Equality of optimal value is coarser because two states may attain the same optimum through different legal continuations. Two first actions with eventual success can have distinct resource costs.

Every certified macro path has a primitive expansion by the MeTTa replay recurrence. With unlimited calls this preserves the physical reachable set. With a one-query macro tariff and a finite call allowance, it weakly expands reachable states relative to primitive-only certification whenever both schedulers can choose primitives. It can strictly expand the set. Two legal consecutive transitions and STOP need three primitive-schedule queries, while the corresponding certified macro and STOP need two. The result concerns available certificates. Policy choices and native engine work determine whether that extra reachability is useful.

Reserve repair acts only on the first unrepaired incomplete STOP and retains a call for terminal verification. Under the same deterministic policy and caps, its pretrigger prefix is identical to standard inference. Every standard success survives because it has no incomplete STOP. The paired gain is the frequency of rescued standard failures in this design. Early spending can alter the prefix. The learned gate can change which incomplete STOPs are repaired. For variants that can affect successes, the paired effect equals the rescue rate minus the regression rate.
